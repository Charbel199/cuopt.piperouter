Why isn't the GPU winning MORE here? Amdahl's law — measured at res 1000 (3.2 mm)

The GPU already crushed its stages: search fell from 20% of the wall time at res 250 to 2% at res 1000; graph build is ×2.2 faster than CPU. What's left is the 76% of wall time that never touches the GPU — the Python octree subdivision and the SciPy dilations / cost fields / grid IO. The scaling law · more wires, wider gap

Scale the fleet: 8 → 50 wires

A real vehicle program routes harnesses, not 8 wires. Same engine bay, fleets of 20/30/50 wires built from the 8 real connectors plus jittered copies (endpoints checked against the occupancy grid), routed sequentially with earlier wires as obstacles — exactly what ROUTE ALL does. The one-time work (octree, cost fields) is cached across the fleet, so what scales is the per-wire search — the part the GPU owns: every added wire costs ~0.9 s on GPU vs ~3.8 s on CPU at 3.2 mm.

The takeaway in workflow terms

At 50 wires and 3.2 mm cells the GPU returns the full harness in 45 seconds; the CPU needs 3.1 minutes — per iteration. An engineer nudging waypoints and re-routing ten times a session waits 7½ minutes on GPU and over half an hour on CPU. And joint optimization (negotiated congestion, in development) re-solves the whole fleet up to 10 rounds per click: ~7 minutes on GPU vs ~half an hour on CPU, per optimization.

The editing loop · drag a connector, watch it re-route

Live re-route: the workflow only one processor can offer

Routing isn't one solve - it's an editing loop: drag a connector or waypoint, the wire re-solves against the warm scene. Measured exactly that way (scene solved once, then each wire re-solved with its endpoint nudged 3 cells; scene caches warm, as in the app). Below ~500 ms a re-route reads as LIVE feedback under the cursor; at 2 s it is a stall on every nudge.

Same patience, more detail

Read the resolution table above at a fixed time budget instead of a fixed resolution: with ~3 seconds per ROUTE ALL, the CPU affords 8 mm cells while the GPU affords 5 mm - 4× more cells resolved for the same wait. With ~7 seconds, the GPU reaches 3.2 mm; the CPU is still at 5 mm. The GPU doesn't just return the same answer faster - at equal patience it routes against a strictly more faithful model of the geometry.

The verdict

It takes both: GPU-shaped algorithms × GPU hardware

Interactive routing at ~2 seconds per bay exists because two things stack: a hierarchical algorithm that shrinks the search, and GPU stages underneath it — Warp voxelization at up to ×170 on every click, cuGraph search at ×10 whenever graphs are large. Remove the algorithm and even the GPU needs 22 s while the CPU needs 4 minutes — and past 13 mm cells nobody finishes at all. Remove the GPU and the pruned production pipeline still loses ground from 11 mm cells on — ×3.2 at 5 mm, ×4.1 at 3.2 mm — with every stage's trend line pointing further in the GPU's favour as scenes grow — and the gap compounds per wire: a 50-wire harness at 3.2 mm is 45 s vs 3.1 minutes. On production-scale CAD — millions of triangles, full-vehicle volumes, joint optimization re-solving every wire per round — the workload moves exactly in the direction where the GPU is already ×10 and pulling away.
